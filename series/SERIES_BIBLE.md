# Kavero — Series Bible

Version 1 · Developing series reference · Prabhjee Kaur

This document consolidates the owner’s character and story direction. It supersedes conflicting earlier character proposals. The series title, new supporting-character names, example dialogue and remaining production choices are proposals, not final approvals. No manuscripts are commissioned by this document.

## 1. Series identity

**Working title: Kavero’s What If? Adventures.** An expandable series of mostly-text, humorous story collections for independent readers around 8–10, with possible appeal through age 12. Kavero is seven to eight. Each book stands alone; his character, relationships, home and recurring habits connect them.

Initial book holders:

1. Kavero’s Brilliant Inventions — first development focus.
2. Kavero, Everyday Detective.
3. Kavero’s Inside-Out Ideas.

These are three books within one expandable series, not three separate series. Later books may explore other parts of his world without replacing the core cast.

The setting is an internationally accessible, comfortable neighbourhood, not specifically India. The country is unspecified. Keep its geography and routines concrete and internally consistent. Do not default to India-specific names or customs, and do not assume a specific Western country either.

## 2. Format and readership

- Target: 12,000 story words per book, with front/back matter additional.
- Recommended structure: 15 stories averaging 800 words. The owner permits roughly 15–20 stories and flexibility if needed. Do not pad prose or add filler stories.
- Print trim: 5.25 × 8 inches. Black-and-white interior, predominantly text.
- Sparse small line drawings, heading motifs or tail pieces. Five to eight drawings per book is an earlier proposal, not a fixed quota.
- Approximately five print pages per story is an aim, not a constraint. Around 75 story pages and roughly 85–90 total pages require confirmation through a sample layout.
- Kindle: reflowable text and occasional inline images; no fixed ebook page count.
- Initial three-book story-word target: 36,000. This is not a cap on the expanding series.

## 3. Kavero: the centre of every story

Kavero is affectionate, playful, slightly mischievous and endlessly inquisitive. He has dark curly hair, soft round cheeks, a slightly plump huggable build, and expressive school-age features. His body is never the joke. The character-sheet explorations establish a direction; the final model remains to be selected. The realistic image is a separate visual exploration.

He is not an academic prodigy who already knows everything. He notices ordinary things and cannot resist asking why they work that way. His questions may be muddled, naïve or wonderfully unexpected. The story treats this with affection rather than calling him stupid.

His central wish is to find answers to all his why, how, where, when and what-if questions. Every answer can produce another question. He asks because he wants to know, not because he intends to disobey or upset anyone.

Excitement makes him skip a step or follow a clue beyond his original purpose. One small investigation leads to another. He can get disappointed, embarrassed or frustrated, then keep trying. He helps repair the consequences, including asking for assistance. People may be exasperated in the moment, but his good intentions remain clear.

**Signature phrase:** “But what if…?” Use naturally, not as a compulsory refrain on every page.

## 4. The What If? diary — corrected definition

This is his indispensable pocket diary of QUESTIONS. It is not a conventional journal of daily events, childhood memories, or a recap of the mess he made that day. The earlier incident-diary interpretation is superseded.

He records things he wants to ask, find out or test: little mysteries, unexpected possibilities, and questions he does not want to forget. Whenever he has a spare moment, he returns to them. He clings to the diary because it holds his unfinished wondering.

He carries it in a pocket. If new clothes have no suitable pocket, Mum and Dad arrange fabric and a sewn-on pocket. This is a warm family accommodation and a recurring visual/detail cue.

Optional supporting device: simple sketches or an answer beside a question. These must not turn the diary back into a daily-events journal. A story may end with a new question, but not every story needs a diary ending.

Illustrative question examples, not fixed story assignments:

- What if a blanket could make the bed by itself?
- Where does the school bell’s sound start?
- Why does a fizzy drink bubble? Could plain water do that too?
- What if my pocket could hold every question I ever asked?
- How would I find out where Santa lives?

These are his questions, not factual claims. Real explanations should be accurate when developed; fantasy or imagination should be distinguishable from real events. The Santa question is not permission to settle a family belief for readers.

## 5. His three friends

New names below are recommended working names; none has been uniqueness-checked. They replace the earlier Ella/Mira and other friend-name suggestions for discussion. All three friends are around Kavero’s age; exact ages remain open. The girl is his best friend, with no romantic relationship implied.

### Poppy — mellow girl best friend; proposed name

Soft-spoken, sweet, mellow and generally accommodating in manner. She is also a firm everyday sceptic of Kavero’s questions: things are the way they are, so why question everything? She does not share his need to investigate. She often contradicts his what-ifs, gently but sincerely.

Her initial instinct is to leave familiar things alone. Her resistance comes from temperament and trust in routine, not lack of intelligence. She can disagree, make choices and occasionally see a practical point the others miss. Do not generalise her outlook to girls as a group.

She and Kavero are very close despite this difference. Their disagreement generates conversation rather than hostility. She owns the cat.

Example voice: “But it already works like that, Kavero.”

### Pip — enthusiastic fellow questioner; proposed name

Kavero’s eager supporter. He loves hearing a new question and usually says yes before understanding the plan. He adds questions of his own that are even more improbable or confused. His delight amplifies Kavero’s momentum.

He is not a punching bag or a child whom the narration mocks. Give his sincere contributions occasional surprising usefulness. He can also have a preference or limit of his own; agreement is his tendency, not his entire humanity.

Example voice: “Yes! And what if we made it twice as big?”

### Toby — cautious peacemaker; proposed name

More balanced and hesitant about taking a leap. He tries to calm disagreements and keep the group together. With Poppy, he tends to agree that things should be left alone; with Kavero, he begins to believe the experiment might work.

This shifting position comes from wanting harmony and reassurance, not deceit. He often sees a practical concern but struggles to say it decisively. He can gradually find the confidence to state his own view.

Example voice: “Maybe we could try the small one first?”

### The group’s comic rhythm

Kavero asks. Poppy doubts the need. Pip makes the possibility bigger. Toby tries to find a manageable middle. Vary this rhythm rather than repeating the same exchange in every story. Friends need not all appear together; smaller combinations reveal different sides of them.

## 6. The occasional younger cousin

**Suggested name: Millie**, replacing the earlier Lucy proposal. A younger cousin, not a nephew or sibling. Earlier age-five suggestion remains provisional. She lives in another household, possibly a few blocks away, and appears on visits rather than being constantly present.

Her exact personality is not settled. One optional contrast is a literal-minded younger child whose simple questions expose a flaw in the older children’s complicated explanations. This is a proposal, not owner-defined canon.

## 7. The puppy and the cat

### Fozzy — Kavero’s puppy; proposed name

Cute, affectionate, bouncy and interested in everything through sniffing, licking and chewing. Slippers and balls attract him. Show mouthing, carrying or chewing non-food objects rather than treating swallowing them as harmless comedy.

When Kavero is upset or makes a sad face, Fozzy clambers or leaps onto him and licks him until he bursts into giggles. This is a recurring affectionate beat, not an automatic replacement for solving every emotional problem.

The cat intimidates him. When flustered, he seeks reassurance and may lick everyone even more. After an experiment goes wrong, he tries to investigate or lick the spill; the family promptly stops him when it is not suitable. Humour comes from eagerness and interruption, not illness or injury.

### Miette — Poppy’s cat; proposed name, pronounced mee-ET

An aloof, unreadable cat who appears to consider everyone else rather beneath him. He spends much of his time sitting, sleeping, watching or occupying a high place. Shelves and inconvenient vantage points suit him especially well.

He follows the children despite seeming indifferent. He settles exactly where something needs to happen, interrupts a pause with an ill-timed purr, or contributes a long cold stare. Nobody can reliably tell what he thinks or wants. Do not routinely translate his thoughts into narration; his mystery is part of the joke.

The apparent superiority is comic interpretation, not proof that he is cruel or representative of all cats. He need not become secretly responsible for every mystery.

### Pet relationship

The children get along beautifully; their pets do not. Enthusiastic puppy affection meets immovable feline disapproval. Use blocked paths, occupied boxes, stolen seats, nervous licking and silent stand-offs. Avoid violent fights, frightening chases or injuries. The pets complicate scenes without taking the story away from Kavero.

Alternative pet names for discussion: **Waffles** for the puppy; **Muffin** for the cat. These are ordinary naming proposals, not claims of exclusive use.

## 8. Family

### Mum

Hardworking, deeply loving and doting. Relaxed and patient with Kavero; does not habitually scold or shout. She may not understand every question or plan, but her affection is unquestioned. Mum has her own work and responsibilities. Loving boundaries and helping clean up are compatible with this warmth.

### Dad

Hardworking, chilled out, and looking forward to weekends as his main time with Kavero. He often does not understand exactly what Kavero is asking or attempting. His instinct is that if his son is all right and enjoying himself, things are fine.

He is not the main scientific explainer or expert making partner; those earlier proposals are superseded. Keep him affectionate and attentive within his limited time rather than depicting him as neglectful.

### Family values and home

A loving, hardworking, comfortable upper-middle-class family. The owner describes them as God-fearing: record faith and reverence as family values without inventing a religion, denomination or country. No fear-based religious lessons are implied.

### Grandma — willing, bewildered assistant

Loving, playful and a good companion. Kavero excitedly brings her questions and recruits her into experiments. She usually does not understand the science or quite what he is about to do, but joins in willingly.

She holds something, follows his directions or accompanies him outside. An innocent mishap might splash water on her face or leave her needing to clean up. She laughs it off; they help tidy and later tell Mum what happened together. Her participation is affectionate and voluntary, not coerced or degrading.

She listens more than she answers. Occasionally she offers a mature life observation that Kavero cannot yet understand, followed by something like “You’ll understand when you’re older.” She is neither an expert scientist nor a hidden source of every solution. The earlier ingenious-Grandma-with-the-answer role is superseded.

## 9. Mr Bell — patient encourager

The neighbour who repairs things. He does not get irritated by Kavero’s questions. He explains kindly and concretely in ways that make sense to Kavero, and encourages him to keep asking.

His answers should sound like a warm exchange, not a lecture. He can demonstrate, use a familiar comparison or help Kavero notice something. He need not solve the whole adventure; Kavero still observes, tries and contributes. Do not invent a fixed profession beyond the established repair role without recording it as a proposal.

## 10. School and curiosity

School can provide impatience, misunderstandings and mild consequences. Kavero may follow the bell sound toward the principal’s office because he is investigating, not deliberately trying to be bad. A quiet time-out may give him room to imagine another possibility.

The owner introduced teachers who send him out or impose punishment, departing from the earliest punishment-free brief. Exact school boundaries are still open. Until resolved, keep consequences non-frightening and non-humiliating; do not make all teachers villains or reward calculated disruption as an escape technique. The earlier teacher name Ms Reed remains provisional.

## 11. Story principles

The flexible engine is an ordinary observation, a question, an enthusiastic attempt, a funny complication, and a discovery or repair. Sometimes a story ends with a new question rather than a solved puzzle. Vary forms, stakes and endings.

Keep the stories warm, funny and concrete. No moral speeches, cruelty, body-shaming, frightening punishment or violence. Small disappointments and mistakes are welcome. Kavero’s curiosity is valuable even when his plan fails.

Real experiments requiring tools, heat, electricity or unfamiliar materials need appropriate adult involvement; the story is not an invitation to imitate hazardous steps. Grandma’s comic mishaps remain harmless and reversible. Pet interruptions remain gentle.

No one must explain every scientific detail. When a factual answer is offered, it should be correct and understandable. Imagination may be extravagant without confusing it with an actual scientific result.

## Owner refinement: Grandma, diary attachment and visual cast

Grandma is exceptionally endearing and treasures as much time with Kavero as possible. She willingly goes along with his baffling explanations and experiments because their time together is what she loves. At home he affectionately talks her ears off, bombarding her with questions and requests. She may struggle to follow his words, but enjoys the companionship. When he is out with friends, she happily relaxes; she has her own comfortable life and is not portrayed as abandoned. Her participation remains voluntary, funny and loving.

Kavero becomes very distressed and urgently searches when he cannot find his What If? diary. Show worry and flustered behaviour, not a clinical diagnosis. His family can reassure him and help search. The diary contains his precious unanswered questions, not a daily-events journal.
