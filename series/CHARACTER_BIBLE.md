# Kavero — Expanded Character Bible

Owner-defined traits are the foundation. The behavioural detail, illustrative dialogue and growth possibilities below are development proposals, not new owner-approved facts. Supporting names remain working names. Readers: 8–10; humour is affectionate and concrete. Appearance references are linked per character.


## Kavero

### Personality and motivation

He wants to answer every question that catches his attention. His What If? diary holds questions he cannot bear to lose, not daily-event entries. Warm, affectionate and physically expressive, he leans toward possibilities before considering practical steps. He is seven to eight, not a miniature scientist.

### How the humour works

He announces a grand solution to a tiny inconvenience, then gives a completely sincere explanation for its unexpected result. Let his confidence wobble; he can ask for help without losing his sparkle. A missing diary causes real worry, followed by a patient search, not a diagnosis or ridicule.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

Poppy questions the need, Pip magnifies the idea and Toby offers a cautious middle. Granny is his cherished assistant, not his servant. Mr Bell makes explanations understandable; his parents make home dependable. Fozzy can turn a sad face into giggles, but cannot solve the underlying problem.

### Writing boundaries

He can become absorbed and interrupt, but notices other people’s discomfort and helps put things right. No smug genius, intentional cruelty, body jokes or compulsory moral speech.

### Voice example — illustrative

“It nearly worked. The nearly part was quite small.”

## Poppy

### Personality and motivation

His mellow, sweet girl best friend values familiar routines. Her gentle manner coexists with sincere disagreement: if something already works, why ask whether it could work differently? She cares about Kavero even when his question makes no sense to her.

### How the humour works

Her humour is quiet understatement while the boys elaborate. She may patiently continue a simple task beside a complicated contraption intended to perform it. Give her good observations and occasional mistaken assumptions.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

She can steady Toby, resist Pip’s escalation and challenge Kavero without hostility. Miette is her cat, but even she cannot interpret every stare. She likes Granny and does not speak for all girls.

### Writing boundaries

Do not make her submissive in every situation, anti-intellectual, always correct or merely the sensible girl who cleans up boys’ mistakes. Her scepticism concerns a particular idea, not whether Kavero deserves affection.

### Voice example — illustrative

“Could we try the ordinary way before we improve it?”

## Pip

### Personality and motivation

An eager supporter and fellow questioner, Pip loves the excitement of possibility. He often agrees before understanding. His extra questions are even more improbable because he builds on whatever just sounded exciting.

### How the humour works

He volunteers the largest container, celebrates a test before it finishes, or asks whether an invention can do something utterly unrelated. His sincerity is the joke, never a narrator calling him stupid.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

With Kavero he accelerates a plan. Poppy makes him explain what he actually means. Toby helps him scale down. He enjoys Granny joining in and may mistake her cheerful nod for technical agreement.

### Writing boundaries

He must sometimes contribute a useful unexpected connection and have preferences of his own. Do not make him expendable comic relief, reckless toward pets or incapable of learning.

### Voice example — illustrative

“Yes! Could it do Tuesdays as well?”

## Toby

### Personality and motivation

A kind, cautious peacemaker who wants everyone to feel comfortable. He can see both sides, but companionship influences his opinion: Poppy makes caution convincing and Kavero makes the adventure tempting.

### How the humour works

He agrees with two incompatible proposals, then tries to invent a polite compromise. A careful suggestion can arrive just after everybody has started the bigger experiment.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

He reassures Poppy that they can stop and assures Kavero they can try. Pip tests his ability to say no. With Millie, he becomes a considerate older companion. Mr Bell’s clear explanation gives him confidence.

### Writing boundaries

His caution is not cowardice, and changing his mind is not deceit. Give him occasions to express his own view and be listened to. Do not make him permanently anxious.

### Voice example — illustrative

“I agree. Mostly. With the smaller version.”

## Millie

### Personality and motivation

The younger cousin lives in another household and visits occasionally. Around five is a design proposal, not a final age lock. Her proposed personality is literal-minded, affectionate and eager for a concrete job.

### How the humour works

She follows the exact words rather than the intended meaning, asks the simplest question in the room or takes a pretend label very seriously. Her small contribution can reveal a flaw.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

Kavero enjoys being the older guide; one friend can join these episodes. Granny includes her patiently. She has her own home and does not become an ever-present fourth friend.

### Writing boundaries

No baby-talk caricature, endless tantrums or using her as a danger magnet. Give understandable tasks and preserve her dignity.

### Voice example — illustrative

“You said every sock. This one is a sock.”

## Granny

### Personality and motivation

Extremely endearing and loving, Granny joins in because she wants time with Kavero. She rarely understands the science but willingly holds, pours, waits or listens. She happily relaxes when he is with friends; she is not lonely on command.

### How the humour works

She earnestly follows a baffling instruction, holds the wrong end or waits for a signal that Kavero forgot to give. A harmless splash can leave her laughing and wiping her glasses. The joke is shared, not at her expense.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

Kavero bombards her with questions at home; she responds with affection and play. They tidy together and tell Mum about their day. Mr Bell can supply explanations without making Granny seem foolish.

### Writing boundaries

She is neither a scientific solver nor a servant. She can ask for a break. Avoid humiliating slapstick, injury or guilt about leaving her. Sometimes her life wisdom goes over Kavero’s head.

### Voice example — illustrative

“Am I holding the important end, darling?”

## Mum

### Personality and motivation

Hardworking, doting and relaxed, Mum understands the child better than every question he asks. She makes affection reliable while still having responsibilities of her own. Faith is a family value; no religion or denomination is specified.

### How the humour works

She arrives after the most complicated explanation and asks a perfectly practical question. She can recognise that an ordinary towel is now labelled an invention without becoming sarcastic or dismissive.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

She supports Kavero’s pocket-diary habit, shares affectionate parenting with Dad and listens to Granny and Kavero’s reports. Friends feel welcome, while household needs remain real.

### Writing boundaries

No shouting or harsh scolding; gentle boundaries and shared cleaning are appropriate. Do not make her the only person who cleans, an omniscient explainer or always available.

### Voice example — illustrative

“Tell me the short version while we find a cloth.”

## Dad

### Personality and motivation

Hardworking and chilled out, Dad looks forward to weekends with Kavero. He often does not grasp the question or mechanism but enjoys his son’s enthusiasm. He is a loving presence, not the main science teacher.

### How the humour works

He confidently offers to be a test customer before discovering what the product does. His relaxed response can puncture an unnecessarily grand announcement with warmth.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

He and Mum support practical needs such as adding a diary pocket. He can join Granny’s testing and enjoy the children’s show. Mr Bell’s technical role stays distinct.

### Writing boundaries

Do not turn relaxed parenting into neglect. Show him listening and responding when help is needed. Weekends are his main shared time, not a ban on every other interaction.

### Voice example — illustrative

“All right. What am I volunteering for this time?”

## Mr Bell

### Personality and motivation

The repair-minded neighbour is patient with questions and genuinely encourages wondering. He gives concrete answers with familiar objects or comparisons. His exact employment history is unspecified.

### How the humour works

His calmly simple observation can sit beside an extravagant machine. He may inspect a crooked wheel while the children debate a much grander explanation.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

Kavero trusts him because his words make sense. Toby gains confidence, Poppy can ask practical follow-ups, and Pip can add an odd question without being mocked. He respects Granny’s contribution.

### Writing boundaries

No long lectures or fixing every story himself. He may say they need to test something rather than pretending certainty. Adult supervision is natural where relevant.

### Voice example — illustrative

“Good question. What do you notice when we turn it slowly?”

## Ms Reed

### Personality and motivation

The teacher is a developing supporting role. Name and appearance are working references. She has a classroom to manage and can be impatient with interruptions without being cruel. The owner’s exact time-out and consequence approach remains unresolved.

### How the humour works

School routines and Kavero’s literal curiosity can collide. She asks for an answer to one question while he is already investigating another.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

She should see his good intentions even when redirecting him. Friends may respond differently to classroom rules. Do not invent a relationship with the family or Mr Bell without recording it.

### Writing boundaries

Until school boundaries are settled, use mild non-humiliating redirection. No frightening punishment, teacher-villain stereotype or deliberate disruption as a rewarded escape strategy.

### Voice example — illustrative

“That is another question, Kavero. Keep it safe for later.”

## Fozzy

### Personality and motivation

Kavero’s puppy is affectionate, bouncy and led by his nose and mouth. He licks people, carries slippers and mouths toys. His floppy ears, rounded paws and eager face should remain recognisable.

### How the humour works

He mistakes activity for an invitation, investigates a spill before anyone can explain it or delivers a slipper to the wrong place. Flustered by the cat, he seeks reassurance through even more licking.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

Kavero’s sad face draws affectionate licks and giggles. Granny is another welcoming companion. Miette’s stillness baffles him; comic tension need not become a fight.

### Writing boundaries

No swallowing slippers, harmful spills or unsafe materials as harmless comedy. Adults stop inappropriate licking. He is a puppy, not a talking human or a device that resolves every emotion.

### Voice example — illustrative

Nonverbal: tail, ears, nose, licking, hesitant little approach.

## Miette

### Personality and motivation

Poppy’s cat is aloof, sleepy and unreadable, with an air of dignified superiority. He prefers heights and inconvenient resting places. He follows the children while appearing indifferent to them.

### How the humour works

A purr arrives in a solemn pause; the exact object someone needs becomes his seat. His tiny glance can answer a grand announcement without explaining what it means.

### Relationships and scene behaviour

Poppy loves him but cannot decode him. Kavero invents theories; Fozzy becomes flustered. Toby may politely negotiate with him while Pip assumes the cat has approved a plan.

### Writing boundaries

Do not narrate definite human thoughts, make him genuinely cruel or blame every mystery on him. His inscrutability is the point. Maintain grey/white markings and round feline silhouette.

### Voice example — illustrative

Nonverbal: long stare, slow blink, small purr, turned back, deliberate settling.
